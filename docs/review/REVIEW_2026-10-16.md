# Review 2026-10-16: Trends, classification, social media, engagement, Google Trends

Requested by the owner on 15 October 2026. The review covers five questions:
1. The Trends page: the need line sits near 0, and the y axis does not match article counts.
2. Better classification, with users able to train the classifier.
3. More social media.
4. Engagement measures.
5. Google Trends as a public-interest signal.

## How the review was done

- **Data:** a redacted copy of the production database, taken 16 October 2026 with no user accounts or sessions. All the numbers below come from it.
- **Investigators:** six worked in parallel:
  - Trends data;
  - Trends design and statistics;
  - classification root causes;
  - social media;
  - engagement;
  - Google Trends.
  The research agents cited sources dated 2025–2026. Each investigator was then checked by an independent verifier who tried to refute every finding. Where a verifier corrected a claim, the corrected version is used here.
- **Blind labelling:** four annotators labelled a stratified sample of 532 articles without seeing the machine's themes. The labels are in `docs/review/gold_labels_2026-10-16.jsonl`.
  - 120 items were double-labelled. Agreement was κ = 0.93 for themes and 0.94 for "about Libya".
  - The annotators were language-model agents, so this is a silver standard. A human spot-check of about 50 items, mostly the gender ones, should come before any threshold is treated as final.

## 1. Trends

### Why the need line sits at 0

There are four separate causes.

1. **Lenses with no data.** Events and journalist need are 0 everywhere. Humanitarian need is 0 for 12 of 13 themes, yet its button stayed enabled because 4 cells from 2025 had once been scored.
   - The lens choice is remembered in the browser. Anyone who once clicked Humanitarian saw 13 flat lines.
2. **A Wikipedia collector bug.** An article listed under two themes was only ever credited to the first theme that collected it.
   - "Gender: legal status" shares *Women in Libya* with "violence against women". It got 1 page view in 2026.
   - "Migrant women" shares *Human trafficking in Libya* with migration, so it only gets its Arabic article.
   - "Gender: economic & health" has no Wikipedia articles configured at all.
3. **Indexing to the mean.** Each line was divided by its own 52-week average (= 100).
   - A theme with an average of 0.5 articles per 4 weeks jumps to 300–680 when one article appears. That sets the top of the axis and pins the other line to the floor.
   - One stray page view sent "legal status" to 5,200.
4. **The last week was incomplete,** and in opposite directions for the two lines.
   - Wikipedia data stops the day before yesterday, so need was too low.
   - Coverage included live collection, so it was too high.
   - Every panel therefore ended with coverage above need.

### Why the y axis did not match the articles

The axis was an index with no label. The tooltip printed a different number again: raw 4-week coverage from *all* sources, including the backfill.

The line counted a third set (outlets present all year), and the score used a fourth (a 16-week set). Four different counts belonged to one point.

Example, security in the week of 12 October:
- the tooltip said 523 articles;
- the line used 218;
- the axis showed 125.9.

### Is the approach sound?

Not as built.

- **The picture and the score disagreed.** "Blue below grey" (blue = coverage, grey = need) matched the sign of the gap score in only 65% of scored weeks. In 96 weeks the blue line was more than 1.5 times below the grey one, and the app called 53 of those "covered as expected or more".
- **Indexing hides magnitude and uses hindsight.** A theme with 0.5 articles a month and one with 173 both sit around 100. Old points also move as new weeks arrive.
- **Wikipedia page views are a weak "need" signal for Libya.**
  - Views are 84% English and come from readers worldwide.
  - Health gets about 5 views a day, so it is never scored.
  - One long-standing reference article supplies 81–94% of the views for about half the themes.
  - Week-to-week changes in views barely move with coverage.
- **The shrinkage prior was set in page views (m = 2,000).** In article terms it ranged from 1.5 to 1,800 articles. Migration and most gender themes could never reach the "gap" pill (their maximum possible gap was about 0.22–0.25).
- **Coverage drifts with collection.** All Google News history was collected on 12–15 October by date-range search, and Google returns more for recent weeks. The same 112 "steady" outlets give 182 articles a week at the start of the year and 391 at the end.
  - GDELT, which does not depend on what we collect, is flat over the same period.
  - So the recent negative gaps are partly an artefact.

See "What was built" at the end.

## 2. Classification

### How good is it today (blind labels)

| | Precision | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| All active theme links, uniform random sample (n = 160) | 0.61 | 0.73 (6 general themes) |
| Links where a rule term is in the **title** | 0.72–0.77 | |
| Links where the rule term is only in the **snippet** | 0.39–0.44 | |
| Links that exist only because a **search or alert query** returned the item | 0.40 (own query), 0.23 (another theme's query) | |
| Gender links from co-occurrence pairs found in snippets | 0.23 | |

- 39% of all active theme links (23,105 of 59,614 since 2024) existed only because a search or alert query returned the item.
- Gender recall cannot be measured from this sample: only 1 gender item appeared in 160 random items. A targeted recall sample is needed.
- The Libya relevance guard is precise (0.97). But it wrongly drops about a quarter of what it filters. One cause is a normaliser bug that turns "Libya's" into "libyas", which affected 1,087 articles.

### Your two examples

1. **"Torrential rains in Libya's Tripoli kill two" under women's political participation.** It came through the Google Alert for that theme.
   - The only rule hit was "women's representation". That phrase sat in the site's navigation text, which Google Alerts put into the snippet ("… Women's Representation . Other News to Consider …").
   - Google builds alert snippets around the alert's own query words: 82% of alert items contain the query term in the snippet. So the strict re-check of gender themes was circular for alerts.
2. **"Türkiye recovers black box from jet crash that killed Libyan military chief" under Security and Governance.**
   - Security came from the generic word "killed".
   - Governance had no text evidence at all. A second Google News query (governance) returned the same URL, and the pipeline then added that theme as an active label.

### What makes classification better

**Rule and pipeline fixes**
- Treat a search or alert theme as a hint the classifier can use, never as a label on its own.
- Weight the title over the snippet.
- Clean snippets: alert keyword excerpts, WordPress "The post … appeared first on …", HTML entities, agency datelines.
- Keep co-occurrence pairs within a short window.
- Add a veto context, so "killed" in a crash, flood or traffic accident is not "security".
- Tidy generic terms.
- Fix the possessive bug.

**A learned classifier**
- Per-theme logistic regression on character and word n-grams, trained on the rule tags (weak supervision). A second small model is trained only on human labels and blended in.
- On the blind labels this lifted macro-F1 from 0.62 to 0.72 on the six general themes:
  - health precision 0.36 → 0.68;
  - services 0.50 → 0.76;
  - migration 0.64 → 0.82.
- It cannot learn the gender themes from rule tags alone: there are only 0–263 examples. So for gender the model may only remove a rule tag, and only once enough human labels exist.

**Users training it**
- Corrections mostly help through the small model trained on human labels.
- The simulation found that about 50 well-chosen labels, picked where the model is least sure, match the rules on gender themes. Picking at random takes about 200.
- So the app needs:
  - a review queue that shows the items the model is least sure about;
  - labels that record the whole theme set, not one theme at a time;
  - a nightly retrain with a promotion gate.

## 3. Social media

- **Today:** social posts arrive only by accident. Of 381 items, all came through Google Alerts or Google News. There is no social collector.
- **Personal accounts:** 61 counted items come from unlabelled named or numeric accounts, which can be private individuals.

### Options by platform

| Platform | Route | Verdict |
|---|---|---|

## 4. Engagement

- **Facebook link engagement (Graph API).** This is the one official, article-level signal that matches where Libyans read news. It needs a Meta developer app, which you would create; no app review is needed for this endpoint.
  - The counts are deliberately approximate, and each URL can be queried at most 10 times an hour.
  - We already hold 2023–24 Facebook engagement per outlet from the social-media scrape. It shows very large differences between outlets, so engagement must always be read against each outlet's own usual level.
- **Our own links are the first blocker.** 60% of counted articles carry only a Google News redirect, so URL-based services cannot match them. Weighted by where the Facebook engagement is, though, about 76% of the expected signal already sits on direct URLs.
- **Free signals we can compute ourselves:**
  - story pickup, meaning how many outlets ran the same story (88.5% of stories appear in one outlet only);
  - outlet web rank from Tranco.
  Web rank does not track Libyan reach, because Arabic outlets live on Facebook.
- **Not worth building:** Bluesky (1 matched URL in 12 weeks), Reddit (blocked without approval), Hacker News (0 hits), X (paid, small audience), SharedCount (resells the same Facebook numbers), and on-site view counters (some increment when read).

## 5. Google Trends

**Feasible for some themes.** Libyans search in Arabic, and Google holds 98% of searches in Libya.

- **Themes that work:** themes that are part of daily life give full weekly series: electricity and water, dinar, salaries, cash and petrol, hospitals.
- **Themes that don't:** migration, every gender theme and most districts fall below Google's privacy threshold.
- **Event themes:** for clashes, searches and coverage spike in the same weeks, so no gap appears.

**Access**
- The official Google Trends API is still a gated alpha. You can apply.
- pytrends was archived in April 2025.
- The website's own data endpoints work from a normal PC at a polite rate. Google tags these sessions as scrapers, so they must never run from the server.
- Paid providers are cheap at our volume (SerpApi has a free tier), but Google is suing SerpApi.

**How it works and its caveats**
- Values are integers from 0 to 100, scaled to the largest term in each request. Comparing themes therefore needs an everyday anchor term in every request.
- Some search words pick up other things. «الانتخابات» peaks for the Turkish and US elections.
- Google revises the last 2 weeks.
- There is a data-collection change on 1 January 2022.
- Comparing a theme's share of searches with its share of articles depends on which words are in each theme's list. That comparison should never be scored. Each theme should be compared with its own history instead.

**Literature:** search data is a partial, issue-specific measure of attention (Mellon 2013 and 2014; Ripberger 2011). News drives searches, so a gap means searches rose while coverage did not.

## What was built (16 October 2026)

Six packages were built in parallel. Each one was reviewed by an independent agent and then fixed. They were merged, reviewed again as a whole and fixed again, then rehearsed end to end on a copy of the production database before deploying.

### Trends
- **New chart.** The small multiples are now *articles per week, theme by theme*:
  - bars on an axis labelled "articles a week";
  - a grey step line for the usual level, worked out from the lens's need data;
  - a strip under each chart that turns red when the last 4 weeks fell clearly short (1.5 times fewer or worse).
- **Tooltips and click-throughs.** The tooltip shows the numbers that are drawn, and clicking a bar lists exactly the articles in it. On the rehearsal, 78 bars were checked and none differed.
- **Header wording.** It reads, for example, "About 1.9 times fewer articles than usual", "About as usual" or "About 1.6 times more articles than usual". Where a week is not judged, it says why: still coming in, too few articles expected, no reader data, or still building a baseline.
- **Fixes to the scoring:**
  - The current week is never judged, because it is still coming in.
  - The shrinkage prior is now counted in articles, not page views. This lets small and gender themes reach a red gap.
  - The evidence minimum is now "3 articles expected" rather than "500 page views". Health is now scored.
  - The set of outlets that counts the same across time is shared between the score and the chart, and tolerates two quiet weeks.
  - Wikipedia articles listed under two themes now count for both, so legal status and migrant women have data.
  - A one-day spike of more than 5 times the usual is capped, which stops bot traffic counting as interest.
- **Lens availability.** A lens is only offered when it has recent judged data, so Humanitarian no longer draws flat zero lines.
- **Lens name.** The audience lens is renamed "International readers (Wikipedia)", because its page views are worldwide and mostly English.
- **Monthly bars** are stacked by source, with the current month hatched.
- **GDELT line.** It averages over calendar days and breaks where data is missing.

### Classification
- **Search and alert results are now only a hint.** A search or alert result no longer makes an article part of a theme on its own. Unconfirmed links are kept as inactive hints. This fixes both of your examples:
  - the Tripoli rains story is no longer under women's political participation;
  - the jet-crash story is no longer under governance;
  - "killed" in a crash, flood or traffic accident no longer counts as security.
- **Title first.** A word found only in the summary needs a second word to count. Summaries are cleaned of alert keyword excerpts, "The post … appeared first on …", HTML entities and agency datelines.
  - The datelines alone had put about 1,600 national-government stories into Tripoli.
- **Smaller rule fixes:**
  - Gender word pairs must sit within 6 words of each other.
  - Some generic terms were replaced with phrases.
  - "Libya's" now matches Libya, which brought back about 1,100 articles.
- **A learned classifier.** It adds the recall the stricter rules lose: one logistic regression per theme on word and character patterns, retrained nightly from people's labels.

  On the blind labels, six general themes:

  | | Precision | Recall | F1 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Before today | 0.51 | 0.80 | 0.62 |
  | After the rule fixes | 0.81 | 0.52 | 0.63 |
  | With the model | 0.72 | 0.76 | 0.73 |

- **Gender themes stay on the word lists.** The model may only remove a gender tag once that theme has 100 labels from people, at least 10 of them positive, and does better than the lists. My machine-made review labels never count towards that.
- **Users train it:**
  - "Label this" on every article records the whole set of themes.
  - A "Help the classifier learn" queue on Contribute shows the articles the model is least sure about.
  - The nightly retrain only goes live if it does no worse than the current model.
  - Admin has a theme-classifier card with every version and one-click rollback.
  - Each article shows why each theme is there, for example "title: killed", "found by a search" or "model 0.82".

### Social media
- **Google-indexed posts.** We now collect posts Google News has indexed for the 106 Facebook register pages and other listed accounts. Each night covers the two previous days. English accounts are also queried in the English edition.
  - A 3-day test added about 470 counted posts, mostly in Tripoli, Benghazi, Jabal al Akhdar and Murqub.
- **YouTube.** Feeds for 9 channels linked from the outlets' own websites. Today the feeds were down from here, so they have their own health line.
- **Not counted:**
  - posts from unlisted accounts, which can be private individuals;
  - whole-platform buckets;
  - link shorteners.
- **Outlet families.** A page's post and the same story on its website count once.
- **Not built:** Telegram (its terms ban scraping for AI use, and Libyan channels are tiny), X (paid) and paid Facebook scraping.

### Engagement
- **Story pickup.** How many outlets carried the same story, shown on each article and available as a sort.
- **Links resolved.** Google News redirect links are resolved to the outlet's own article, so URL-based engagement can work.
- **Facebook link engagement** is built and waits for a Meta app.
- **Monthly web rank** from Tranco for Libyan outlets.

### Google Trends
- **A "Libyan searches (Google)" lens** for economy, services, health and governance. It covers 5 years of weekly data, each theme measured against its own history.
- **Theme coverage.** Migration and the gender themes fall below Google's privacy threshold, and the page says so.
- **How it is collected.** It is fetched on a local machine with `backend/scripts/fetch_gtrends.py`, which is polite and stops at the second rate-limit, then imported. It never runs from the server.

## Decisions I took on your behalf (all reversible)

- **Search and alert themes became hints, not labels.**
  - Today's links were 61% precise, and 38% of them existed only because a search returned the article.
  - The model wins back the recall the stricter rules lose.
- **Gender themes stay on the word lists until people have labelled them.**
  - My 532 blind labels are machine-made, so they never open a gender veto.
  - They do train the general themes and set their thresholds.
- **The audience score kept its own-history design.**
  - A share-based score was prototyped. It was rejected because one theme's surge makes every other theme look short.
- **Red starts at 1.5 times fewer articles than usual.**
  - Contribute and the heatmap still use their earlier, lower bar (1.15 times).
- **The audience lens is now called "International readers (Wikipedia)".**
- **Google-indexed social posts count as coverage, under their own source label.**
  - They stay out of comparisons over time until they have a year of history.
- **The 106 Facebook register pages were given scope "libyan".**
  - Confidence is "likely", with the 2023–24 scrape as evidence.
  - NoonPost and iraqialivetv were marked international, and so were the US Embassy page and alkofiya.tv, which is the Palestinian channel.
- **Not built:**
  - Telegram (its terms ban scraping for AI use);
  - X and paid Facebook scraping (cost);
  - Bluesky and Reddit (no audience or blocked);
  - on-site view counters, some of which count up when you read them.
- **Google Trends data was fetched from this PC.**
  - 16 polite requests, which stopped at the second rate-limit.
  - Google labels these sessions as scrapers. It is public aggregate data, but if you would rather use only the official API, delete the search lens data with `import-search` of an empty file and say so.
